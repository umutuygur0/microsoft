# Gas Compressor Maintenance

## Overview
Reciprocating and centrifugal gas compressors are among the most
mechanically stressed equipment on a gas field: they run continuously,
handle flammable gas at high pressure, and rely on close-tolerance moving
parts to maintain a seal against that pressure. Routine maintenance exists
to catch the early signs of wear — rising vibration, falling seal gas
pressure, creeping temperatures — before they become an unplanned shutdown
or, worse, a gas release from a failed seal or casing.

## Key Safety Precautions
- Isolate and depressurise the compressor and apply lockout/tagout (LOTO)
  before any work begins; a compressor that "looks" stopped can still be
  under full line pressure.
- Hot surfaces and rotating parts remain hazardous well after shutdown;
  allow the machine to cool and confirm rotation has fully stopped before
  approaching moving parts.
- Vent trapped gas from cylinders, casings, and seal cavities to a safe
  location before opening any joint — gas can remain trapped in pockets even
  after the main line pressure reads zero.

## Routine Checks
A compressor's health is read primarily through trends, not single readings.
The following parameters should be logged and compared against both their
alarm/trip set points and their own recent history:
- Lubricating oil level, pressure, and temperature — a slow decline in oil
  pressure often precedes a bearing problem by days or weeks.
- Vibration readings against the alarm and trip set points, watched for
  gradual increases as much as for sudden spikes.
- Seal gas and vent system operation, since a degrading seal typically shows
  rising vent flow before it shows an external leak.
- Cooling water or air flow and temperature, because compressors that run
  hot age their lubricants and seals faster than the maintenance schedule
  assumes.
- Suction and discharge pressures and temperatures at each stage, compared
  against the compressor's performance curve to catch fouling or valve wear.

## Working Procedure
1. Record baseline running parameters before shutdown so that post-maintenance
   performance can be compared against the pre-maintenance condition.
2. Isolate, depressurise, and lock out the machine following the site LOTO
   procedure.
3. Inspect valves, rings, and seals according to the maintenance schedule,
   paying particular attention to any component flagged by recent trend data.
4. Check and top up lubricating oil; replace filters as due, and inspect
   used oil and filter elements for metal particles that might indicate
   internal wear.
5. Verify instrument and trip functions on test, including high-vibration,
   low-oil-pressure, and high-temperature trips — a trip that does not
   function is a protection layer that does not exist.
6. Complete a controlled restart and confirm all parameters return to within
   normal operating limits before leaving the machine unattended.

## Common Failure Modes
- Valve failures (reciprocating compressors) usually announce themselves as
  a change in discharge temperature or a knocking sound before they fail
  completely — early investigation avoids consequential damage to the piston
  and cylinder.
- Seal gas system faults on centrifugal compressors are frequently the root
  cause of an unplanned shutdown; seal gas panel filters and pressure
  differentials deserve as much attention as the compressor itself.
- Fouling of internal passages by liquid carry-over from upstream separation
  reduces efficiency gradually, often mistaken for normal ageing rather than
  a fixable upstream problem.
