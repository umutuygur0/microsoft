# Emergency Shutdown (ESD) Procedure

## Overview
The Emergency Shutdown system exists to bring an entire facility, or a
defined section of it, to a safe state within seconds of an abnormal or
hazardous event — a major gas release, a fire, or a loss of process control
that could otherwise escalate. ESD is deliberately a blunt instrument: it
prioritises isolating and depressurising as fast as possible over preserving
equipment or production, because in the moments after an ESD trip there is
no time to make a nuanced judgement call. The system is designed so that any
person, not just the control room operator, can trigger it the moment they
recognise a hazard.

## Key Safety Precautions
- Personnel safety takes priority over equipment protection at all times —
  no one should hesitate to activate ESD out of concern for restart costs or
  production loss.
- Never reset an ESD trip until the root cause has been identified and
  cleared; resetting a trip without understanding why it activated risks
  walking straight back into the same hazard.
- Assume all lines may still contain pressure until proven otherwise by
  direct verification, even after the blowdown sequence has apparently
  completed.

## When to Activate
- A confirmed uncontrolled gas release, whether from a leak, a failed seal,
  or an open line.
- Fire or explosion anywhere on site, regardless of scale.
- Detection of gas at or above 60% of the Lower Explosive Limit (LEL) in an
  occupied area — this threshold is set well below the point of ignition to
  give a safety margin.
- Loss of critical control or instrument air, since many safety-critical
  valves rely on instrument air to fail to a safe position, and a loss of
  supply can itself trigger a cascading set of process upsets.

## Working Procedure
1. Activate the nearest ESD push button, or trigger the shutdown logic
   directly from the control room if the hazard has already been identified
   there.
2. Confirm the shutdown valves (SDVs) have closed and the affected field
   area is isolated — do not assume closure from the initiating action
   alone; verify against field indication.
3. Confirm the blowdown sequence has depressurised the affected sections to
   the flare system, bringing pressure down to a safe level rather than
   simply stopping flow.
4. Account for all personnel at the designated muster point; this step runs
   in parallel with the technical shutdown, not after it.
5. Notify the shift supervisor and, if the situation warrants it, external
   emergency services without delay.
6. Do not re-enter the affected area until the atmosphere has been confirmed
   safe by gas testing — visual inspection from a distance is not a
   substitute for a gas test at the point of entry.

## Reset
- Reset only on written authorisation from the responsible authority, issued
  after the root cause has been investigated and corrected.
- Perform a controlled, staged restart following the site start-up
  procedure — an ESD reset is not the same action as a normal start-up, and
  bringing everything back online simultaneously reintroduces the risk of
  overlooking a fault that has not actually been fixed.

## Why the Sequence Matters
The order of these steps is deliberate: isolation and depressurisation come
before investigation, because the priority is removing the hazard's energy
source first and understanding it second. Facilities that skip or reorder
steps under pressure to "get back online faster" are the ones most likely to
experience a repeat event from the same unresolved cause.
