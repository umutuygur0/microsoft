# Fire Response

## Overview
A fire on a gas installation is fundamentally different from a fire in most
other industrial settings, because the fuel source is frequently still
flowing. Extinguishing visible flames without stopping the gas supply can
create a far more dangerous situation than the fire itself: an invisible,
odourless cloud of unburned gas that can drift, find an ignition source, and
explode with far greater destructive force than the original fire. This
procedure sets out the immediate actions personnel should take on
discovering a fire, in an order that reflects that priority.

## Key Safety Precautions
- Never extinguish a gas fire unless the gas supply feeding it can also be
  isolated; an unburned gas cloud can re-ignite explosively, and a flame
  that is at least burning off the gas in a controlled way is, perversely,
  often safer than no flame at all until isolation is achieved.
- Raise the alarm before attempting any first-response action — a fire that
  is fought alone and unreported, and then gets out of control, denies the
  wider site the warning time it needs.
- Personal safety and evacuation take priority over property at every stage
  of the response.

## Working Procedure
1. Raise the alarm and activate the nearest fire or ESD call point
   immediately on discovering the fire.
2. If trained and it is safe to do so, isolate the gas supply feeding the
   fire — this single action does more to control a gas fire than any
   extinguisher.
3. Attack only small, incipient fires, and only with the correct
   extinguisher type for the fuel involved.
4. Use dry powder or CO2 on gas and electrical fires; never use water on
   live electrics, and never assume a "small" gas fire is safe to approach
   without confirming the source can be isolated.
5. Evacuate upwind to the muster point if the fire cannot be controlled with
   the equipment immediately at hand.
6. Account for all personnel and report to the incident controller as soon
   as the muster point is reached.
7. Do not re-enter the area until it has been formally declared safe.

## Extinguisher Selection
Using the wrong extinguisher type can make a fire worse rather than better,
so selection matters as much as the decision to fight the fire at all:
- **Gas fire:** dry powder, which interrupts the chemical chain reaction of
  combustion.
- **Electrical fire:** CO2, which does not conduct electricity back to the
  operator and leaves no residue on sensitive equipment.
- **Solid combustibles:** water or foam, which cool the material below its
  ignition temperature.

## After the Fire
Even a fire that is fully extinguished and appears minor should be treated
as a significant event: the underlying cause (a leak, an electrical fault, a
process upset) has not gone away simply because the flames have. The area
should be isolated, made safe, and formally investigated before any
equipment involved is returned to service.
