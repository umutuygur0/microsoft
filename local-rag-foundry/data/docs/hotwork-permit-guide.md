# Hot Work Permit

## Overview
Any work that produces heat, sparks, or open flame — welding, grinding,
cutting, and similar tasks — introduces a reliable ignition source into an
environment where flammable gas may be present. On its own, neither hot work
nor a trace of flammable gas is necessarily dangerous; the hazard exists
specifically at their intersection. The hot work permit system exists to
guarantee that intersection never occurs unmonitored, by requiring a
verified gas-free condition before work starts and continuous vigilance
while it continues.

## Key Safety Precautions
- No hot work may begin until a gas test confirms the area is below 1% LEL —
  this is deliberately a much tighter margin than the general evacuation
  threshold used elsewhere, because hot work is itself an active ignition
  source rather than a passive hazard.
- A dedicated fire watch must be present throughout the work and for 30
  minutes after it stops, since some ignition sources (hot slag, smouldering
  material) can start a fire with a delay after the work itself has ended.
- Remove or cover all combustible material within 15 metres of the work
  location before starting.

## Working Procedure
1. Complete a gas test immediately before issuing the permit — a test from
   earlier in the shift is not valid for a permit issued now.
2. Verify fire extinguishers and a charged hose are in position and readily
   accessible before work begins.
3. Isolate any nearby process lines that could release gas into the work
   area during the job.
4. Brief the fire watch on alarm and evacuation actions specific to this
   job and location, not just generic site knowledge.
5. Perform periodic gas re-tests at the interval stated on the permit — a
   single pass at the start does not guarantee conditions remain safe for
   the duration of the work.
6. Stop work immediately if gas is detected above 1% LEL at any re-test,
   regardless of how close the job is to completion.
7. Maintain the fire watch for 30 minutes after work stops, then formally
   close the permit.

## Why the Fire Watch Continues After Work Stops
Welding and cutting can leave hot slag, sparks lodged in crevices, or
smouldering material that is not visible immediately. A fire that starts
this way can develop 10 or 20 minutes after the welder has packed up and
left, which is exactly why the fire watch requirement extends beyond the
active work period rather than ending the moment the torch is switched off.

## Common Pitfalls
- Treating the initial gas test as valid for the whole shift rather than
  re-testing at the required interval.
- Allowing combustible material to be moved back into the 15 metre zone
  partway through a long job, once the immediate area "looks" clear.
- Closing the fire watch early because the job finished ahead of schedule,
  rather than holding the full 30-minute period regardless of how the work
  went.
