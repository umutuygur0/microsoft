# Gas Leak Detection Procedure

## Overview
Natural gas is colourless and, once odourised, only faintly detectable by
smell at low concentrations — which is why field surveys rely on calibrated
instruments rather than human senses to find leaks reliably. This procedure
describes how to detect and confirm a natural gas leak on a pipeline or
wellhead installation using a calibrated portable gas detector, in a way
that protects the surveyor while producing a result that can be trusted
enough to act on.

## Key Safety Precautions
- Natural gas is highly flammable. Do NOT use any ignition source, mobile
  phone, or non-intrinsically-safe equipment within a suspected leak zone —
  a single spark is enough to ignite an accumulated gas cloud.
- Always approach a suspected leak from upwind, so that any gas present is
  carried away from the surveyor rather than toward them.
- If the reading exceeds 20% of the Lower Explosive Limit (LEL), evacuate
  the area immediately and establish a 50 metre exclusion zone before any
  further action is taken.

## Equipment
- A calibrated portable combustible gas detector, checked within the last
  24 hours and bump-tested before use.
- An intrinsically safe torch for inspecting fittings in low light or
  enclosed spaces.
- Leak detection fluid (a soap-based solution) for pinpointing the exact
  location of a leak once a general area has been identified by the
  detector.

## Working Procedure
1. Confirm the detector is calibrated and has been bump-tested before
   entering the site — an uncalibrated detector produces a reading, but not
   a trustworthy one.
2. Switch on the detector in clean air away from the survey area to
   establish a reliable zero baseline.
3. Walk the survey line slowly, holding the sensor 5 to 10 cm from
   fittings, flanges, and joints — these mechanical connections are by far
   the most common leak points, far more so than the pipe body itself.
4. Note any reading above 5% LEL as a potential leak location requiring
   closer investigation, even though it is below the evacuation threshold.
5. Apply leak detection fluid to the suspected joint; steady bubbling
   confirms escaping gas and also gives a visual indication of leak rate.
6. Record the location, LEL reading, and time on the inspection form, since
   this record feeds directly into the work order and any trend analysis of
   recurring leak points.
7. Isolate the affected section and raise a work order for repair — a
   confirmed leak is not something to monitor and revisit later; it is
   isolated as soon as it is safe to do so.

## Why Fittings and Flanges Matter Most
Straight pipe runs rarely leak in service unless there is external
corrosion or mechanical damage, whereas any mechanical joint — a flange, a
threaded fitting, a valve stem seal — represents a point where two surfaces
must maintain a seal against pressure over years of thermal cycling and
vibration. A systematic survey therefore concentrates disproportionate
attention on these connection points rather than treating the whole
pipeline as equally likely to leak.
