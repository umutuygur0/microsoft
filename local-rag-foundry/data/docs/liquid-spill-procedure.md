# Liquid Spill Response

## Overview
A spill of hydrocarbon condensate, process chemicals, or produced water is
both a safety hazard and an environmental incident from the moment it
occurs — the same spill that creates a flammable vapour cloud near the
source can, if left unchecked, also reach a drain or watercourse and cause
lasting environmental harm well beyond the immediate site. This procedure
covers the immediate response actions that address both risks together,
rather than treating containment and safety as separate concerns.

## Key Safety Precautions
- Hydrocarbon spills create a flammable vapour hazard; eliminate all
  ignition sources in and around the area before doing anything else.
- Wear chemical-resistant PPE appropriate to the specific substance spilled
  — the right glove or suit material for one chemical can be entirely wrong,
  and unsafe, for another.
- Do not enter a vapour cloud without gas monitoring, even to perform an
  action that seems urgent, such as reaching a valve to stop the source.

## Working Procedure
1. Raise the alarm and, if it is safe to do so, stop the source of the
   spill — stopping the source is always more valuable than containing an
   ever-growing pool.
2. Eliminate ignition sources in and around the spill area, including
   vehicles, electrical equipment, and any hot work in progress nearby.
3. Contain the spill using booms, bunds, or absorbent materials, working
   from the outside edges inward to stop it spreading further.
4. Prevent the spill from reaching drains, watercourses, or soil, since
   these pathways turn a contained, recoverable spill into a much larger
   environmental incident.
5. Recover the liquid using pumps or absorbents into approved containers,
   rather than allowing it to evaporate, soak into the ground, or wash away.
6. Notify the environmental coordinator and record the spill volume and
   probable cause, since this record is often required for regulatory
   reporting as well as internal investigation.
7. Dispose of contaminated material — absorbents, soil, protective
   equipment — through an approved waste route rather than general waste.

## Spill Kit Contents
A ready spill kit typically includes absorbent pads and booms, a
non-sparking shovel (to avoid creating an ignition source while handling
contaminated material), drain covers to block nearby drains before a spill
can reach them, and waste bags for contaminated material. Spill kits should
be positioned near likely spill locations — loading points, tank farms,
chemical storage — rather than centrally stored where they may not be
reachable quickly enough.

## Why Speed Matters More Than Scale
A small spill contained within minutes rarely becomes a reportable
environmental incident. The same volume left to spread for even 20 minutes
can reach a drain, soak into ground that then requires remediation, or
create a vapour cloud large enough to represent a genuine ignition hazard.
The first response — stop the source, contain the edges — matters more to
the eventual outcome than any single subsequent step.
