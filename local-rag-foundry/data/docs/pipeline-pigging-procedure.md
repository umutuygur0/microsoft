# Pipeline Pig Launching

## Overview
A "pig" is a device sent through a pipeline to clean it, remove liquid
build-up, or carry inspection instruments along the line, and it is
launched into and received from the pipeline using a purpose-built pressure
vessel called a pig trap. Because the launcher is, functionally, a pressure
vessel with a large removable closure door, the greatest hazard in this
procedure is not the pigging operation itself but the act of opening that
closure — a launcher that is not fully depressurised can turn its own door
into a projectile.

## Key Safety Precautions
- The launcher is a pressure vessel; never open the closure until it is
  confirmed fully depressurised and the pressure gauge reads zero — a gauge
  reading close to zero is not the same as zero, and residual trapped
  pressure has caused serious injuries industry-wide.
- Stand clear of the closure door when opening it, even after confirming
  zero pressure, since trapped pressure in a pocket the gauge does not read
  can still fling the door open unexpectedly.
- Confirm the vent and drain lines are routed to a safe location before
  depressurising, so that any residual liquid or gas is disposed of safely
  rather than released at the work location.

## Working Procedure
1. Confirm the launcher is isolated from the main line and fully
   depressurised before touching the closure.
2. Open the closure and inspect the barrel for cleanliness and any signs of
   internal damage that could affect pig passage.
3. Load the pig into the barrel, confirming its orientation matches the
   manufacturer's markings — a pig loaded backwards may not seal correctly
   against the bore.
4. Close and lock the closure, and confirm the mechanical interlock is
   fully engaged before proceeding to pressurise.
5. Equalise pressure across the launcher and open the kicker line that will
   provide the initial flow to move the pig.
6. Open the main valve and use kicker line flow to launch the pig into the
   main pipeline.
7. Confirm the pig has launched successfully via the signaller (a device
   that detects the pig's passage), then restore the normal valve line-up
   for continued pipeline operation.

## Why the Interlock Matters
Many pig launcher designs include a mechanical interlock that physically
prevents the main line valve from opening while the closure is not fully
locked, and prevents the closure from being opened while the main line
valve is open. This interlock exists as a hardware backup to the procedure
itself — even a well-trained crew following every step correctly benefits
from a physical device that makes the two unsafe states impossible to
create simultaneously.
