# Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) Requirements

## Overview
Personal protective equipment is the last layer of defence in the hierarchy
of hazard control — after elimination, substitution, engineering controls,
and administrative controls have all been applied, PPE protects the
individual from whatever hazard remains. It is not a substitute for those
other controls, but on a gas field, where hazards from flammable
atmospheres, high-noise equipment, and toxic gas can appear even in
well-controlled areas, correctly worn and maintained PPE is what stands
between an incident and a near miss.

## Mandatory PPE (all areas)
- Flame-retardant (FR) coveralls rated to the site standard, worn as the
  baseline layer regardless of the specific task being performed.
- Safety helmet with chin strap, since a helmet that is not secured can be
  dislodged at the exact moment it is needed.
- Safety glasses with side shields, protecting against both frontal and
  peripheral debris.
- Steel-toe, anti-static safety boots, combining impact protection with
  protection against static discharge in flammable atmospheres.
- Hearing protection in designated high-noise zones, defined as areas above
  85 dB — prolonged exposure above this level causes cumulative, irreversible
  hearing damage even without any single dramatic incident.

## Task-Specific PPE
Beyond the baseline requirements, certain tasks carry additional hazards
that require dedicated equipment:
- **Gas surveys:** a personal H2S monitor and combustible gas detector,
  carried by the individual rather than relying solely on fixed detection.
- **Chemical handling:** chemical-resistant gloves and a face shield,
  selected for compatibility with the specific chemical in use.
- **Working at height:** a full-body harness with a certified lanyard,
  inspected before each use.
- **Hot work:** a welding shield, flame-retardant gauntlets, and a fire
  watch, as detailed in the Hot Work Permit procedure.

## H2S Areas
- A personal H2S monitor is mandatory in all sour-gas areas, without
  exception, regardless of how routine the task.
- Escape breathing apparatus must be carried by anyone entering an area
  where H2S concentrations may exceed 10 ppm, providing an immediate means
  of self-rescue.
- Alarm set points are standardised across the site: low alarm at 5 ppm,
  high alarm at 10 ppm — see the H2S Monitoring procedure for the full
  response protocol at each level.

## Inspection
- Inspect all PPE before each use, and remove damaged items from service
  immediately rather than continuing to use equipment that is "probably
  still fine."
- Gas detectors must show a valid calibration date within the last 6
  months; equipment past this date should be treated as unverified rather
  than assumed accurate.

## PPE Is Not "One and Done"
Equipment that protected a worker correctly yesterday is not automatically
safe today: a harness webbing can be nicked by a sharp edge, a coverall can
be contaminated by a chemical splash, a gas detector's battery can be
degrading. Treating the pre-use inspection as a genuine check, rather than a
formality, is what keeps PPE effective throughout its service life rather
than only on the day it was issued.
