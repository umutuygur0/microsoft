# Wellhead Inspection

## Overview
The wellhead and Christmas tree — the assembly of valves and fittings at
the surface of a producing well — form the primary barrier between
high-pressure reservoir hydrocarbons and the surface environment. Routine
inspection confirms that this barrier remains intact and that every valve
in the assembly is in its correct operating position, since a wellhead
component failing or being found in the wrong position is discovered far
better during a scheduled inspection than during an unplanned event.

## Key Safety Precautions
- The wellhead contains high-pressure hydrocarbons at all times during
  production; never loosen fittings under pressure, regardless of how minor
  the apparent leak or how routine the task seems.
- Confirm the master valve and wing valve positions before any intervention
  — these are the primary barriers that can isolate the well entirely, and
  their status must be known with certainty before other work proceeds.
- Wear H2S monitoring equipment in sour wells, since reservoir gas
  composition — and therefore H2S content — can be present even when
  surface equipment shows no other sign of a problem.

## Inspection Steps
1. Check for hydrocarbon leaks at flanges, valve stems, and fittings,
   using the same systematic attention to mechanical joints described in
   the Gas Leak Detection procedure.
2. Record tubing head and casing head pressures, since a change in the
   relationship between these two readings over time can indicate a
   developing integrity issue in the well itself, not just the surface
   equipment.
3. Verify all valves are in their correct operating position according to
   the well's current status (producing, shut in, or undergoing
   intervention).
4. Inspect the pressure gauges for physical damage and confirm they carry a
   valid calibration — a damaged or out-of-calibration gauge can mask a
   genuine pressure problem.
5. Check the grease and hydraulic fluid levels on actuated valves, since
   these systems depend on adequate fluid to develop the force needed to
   operate the valve when commanded.
6. Confirm the surface safety valve (SSV) closes correctly on test — this
   valve is the wellhead's primary automatic protection against an
   uncontrolled release and must be verified functional, not merely present.
7. Inspect the wellhead cellar (the below-grade area around the wellhead)
   for accumulated gas or liquids, since this enclosed space can trap
   heavier-than-air gases that would otherwise disperse at surface level.

## Why the Cellar Check Is Not Optional
A wellhead cellar behaves much like a confined space: it is a below-grade
enclosure where gas can accumulate and where visual inspection from above
may not reveal a hazardous atmosphere underneath. Skipping this check
because "nothing has ever been found there before" removes exactly the
layer of protection that catches a slow leak before it becomes significant.
